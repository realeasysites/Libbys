Septic FAQ

Good questions we hear every week

How often should my septic tank be pumped?

Most households should pump every 3 to 5 years. It depends on tank size, how many people live in the home, and whether you run a garbage disposal. If you don’t know when it was last done, it’s probably time. Call and we’ll help you figure out the right schedule.

What are the warning signs of a septic problem?

Slow drains throughout the house, gurgling pipes, sewage smell in the yard, soggy or extra-green grass over the leach field, or a high-level alarm. Any of these means you should call before it turns into a backup.

Can you pump my tank in the winter?

Often yes, but frozen ground and snow make it harder to reach the cover. That’s why we push fall pumping, and why risers are worth it: the cover sits at ground level, so we can reach it in any season.

What is a septic riser and do I need one?

A riser is a sealed extension that brings your tank’s access lid up to the surface. You skip the digging every time you pump, which saves time and mess and usually saves money over the life of the system.

Do you handle emergencies?

Yes. Backups, alarms and failed pumps are what we’re known for jumping on. Call (207) 767-2614 directly rather than using the form. It’s the fastest way to reach us.
